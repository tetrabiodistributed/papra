To make *The Tetra Biodistributed Project PAPR* (PAPRa) fully adherent
to *Design for Manufacturing (DFM)* and *Design for Assembly (DFA)*
guidelines, several key engineering principles from manufacturing
literature can be integrated into its design.

Because open-source personal protective equipment (PPE) like PAPRa often
begins as a 3D-printed prototype, transitioning it to scalable mass
production requires incorporating design adjustments across assembly
reduction, molding rules, component standardization, and alignment
features:

'''''

==== *1. Part Consolidation and Part Count Reduction*

* *Combine Multi-Piece Housings*: The single most effective way to lower
total manufacturing and assembly costs is to reduce the total number of
parts in an assembly. +
* *Functional Integration*: Merge separate housing shells, battery
brackets, airflow ducts, and filter mounting rings into unified
single-piece components. Integrating functional features into a single
molded part eliminates individual piece-parts, fasteners, and manual
assembly steps.

==== *2. Fastener Elimination and Tool-less Assembly*

* *Incorporate Snap-Fits and Press-Fits*: Threaded fasteners (screws,
nuts, washers) add significant labor costs due to manual alignment and
driving time. Replacing metal screws with integrated *plastic
snap-fits*, *living hinges*, or *press-fit retention tabs* allows
tool-less assembly and quick access for battery or filter changes. +
* *Single-Direction (Top-Down) Assembly*: Configure the internal blower,
circuit board, and wiring harness so they drop into the enclosure in a
straight line along the Z-axis, avoiding complex multi-angle assembly
steps.

==== *3. Transitioning from Additive Prototyping to Injection Molding DFM*

If the respirator housing is scaled from low-volume 3D printing to
mass-production injection molding, specific molding DFM rules must be
applied to the CAD models:

* *Add Draft Angles and Define Parting Planes*: Ensure all vertical
walls have appropriate draft angles in the direction of mold closure to
allow clean part ejection without requiring expensive side-action cores
or slides. +
* *Maintain Uniform Wall Thickness*: Non-uniform wall thicknesses cause
uneven cooling, warping, and long cycle times. Use *multidirectional or
concentric stiffening ribs* and gussets to provide structural rigidity
rather than thick, solid walls. +
* *Eliminate Undercuts and Narrow Features*: Avoid internal and external
undercuts, narrow slots, or sharp projections that complicate mold
tooling and accelerate tool wear.

==== *4. Standardization and Off-the-Shelf (COTS) Hardware*

* *Use Standard Modules*: Ensure that electric blowers, fans, HEPA
filter cartridges, power jacks, and batteries conform to widely
available standard commercial sizes. Designing around standard
commercial parts eliminates the high tooling and production costs of
custom special-purpose hardware.

==== *5. Self-Locating and Error-Proofing (Poka-Yoke) Features*

* *Lead-in Chamfers and Keyways*: Add chamfers, guiding recesses, and
keyway alignment tabs to component interfaces so that operators can
align and insert filters or seals quickly and correctly. +
* *Symmetry vs. Pronounced Asymmetry*: Make parts either completely
symmetric (so they fit in any orientation) or clearly asymmetric (so
they can only be inserted the correct way), preventing assembly errors.

==== *6. Standardized Sealing and Tolerance Optimization*

* *Avoid Unnecessary Tight Tolerances*: Specifying overly tight
dimensions dramatically increases scrap rates and manufacturing costs. +
* *Gaskets over Tight Machining*: Since a PAPR requires airtight seal
integrity, rely on standardized elastomeric O-rings, over-molded
flexible lips, or silicone gaskets to maintain an airtight seal rather
than requiring hyper-precise manufacturing tolerances on rigid housing
parts.

'''''
